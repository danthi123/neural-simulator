# Off-diagonal cross-neuron decorrelation — is there a LOCAL brain-based mechanism that reaches the host ceiling on the real corpus? (deep-research scope; the decision-critical D2 verdict)

**Status:** READ-ONLY deep-research scout (the project's standing "deep research FIRST at a roadblock / before a months-scale commit" move). NO `sim/` edits, NO build, NO GPU. Single deliverable = this doc. **Date:** 2026-06-15. **Author role:** read-only research subagent. Every load-bearing project fact was re-verified against the project's own record (file/line cited) and the surprising mechanism claims were read from the primary sources, not trusted from a prior summary. **This is a scoping/decision doc, NOT a brain-based result and NOT a commitment to build.**

**Question (verbatim scope):** on the project's spiking substrate, recovering paradigmatic category structure from the real TinyStories co-occurrence matrix decomposes into a DIAGONAL half (per-feature normalization, solved locally, real ceiling ~+0.22–0.31) and an OFF-DIAGONAL half (cross-neuron low-rank decorrelation, the piece that reaches host +0.437 ≈ +0.442). Is there a LOCAL, biologically-plausible mechanism that realizes the OFF-DIAGONAL on a spiking point-or-dendritic substrate? And — decision-critically — **does the in-progress D2 build's per-compartment DIVISIVE-gain design address the off-diagonal at all, or is it fundamentally diagonal-only** (meaning D2 as-specified caps at ~49% of host on real, even if its Phase-2 forward-pass NEGATIVE were fixed)?

---

## 0. Terms (defined once)

- **diagonal half of whitening** — per-feature (per-hub) normalization: subtract/divide each hub by its own marginal statistic. Operates on each feature *independently*. Realized locally by subtractive spike-frequency adaptation (`input_mean_adapt`, shipped) or a per-compartment divisive gain (`r_h = x_h/(σ+g_h)`, the D1/D2 mechanism). Mathematically: scaling/centering by a **diagonal** matrix `D`.
- **off-diagonal half of whitening** — removal of the *cross-feature / cross-neuron* correlations: the part of the covariance that couples *different* dimensions. Mathematically: the off-diagonal entries of `Σ^{-1/2}` (or the rotation `V` in `Σ^{-1/2}=V diag(...) Vᵀ`). A diagonal `D` cannot produce it; it requires a matrix that *mixes* dimensions.
- **point neuron** — one somatic membrane state; all input summed at one compartment before the spike (the project's substrate). Can apply one global gain / threshold, not per-input-pair operations.
- **recurrent lateral** — connections *between* the output units (directly, or via a separate interneuron population) carrying each unit's activity to the others; the canonical substrate for cross-neuron decorrelation.
- **Mikulasch-Priesemann limit** — Mikulasch, Rudelt, Priesemann, *PNAS* 2021 (PMC8685685): inhibition must locally balance feedforward input *in individual dendritic compartments* to learn efficient (decorrelated) codes; a point neuron with a single global inhibitory pool cannot, because it lacks per-input knowledge of the population code.
- **host ceiling** — the offline PPMI + truncated-SVD cosine on the same counts: **+0.442** on the real corpus (the data-carries-it reference, never a deliverable). Equivalently reachable by **low-rank ZCA (rank-8) = +0.437** (the project's own CYCLE-73 probe).

## 1. DIAGNOSIS — the gap, in plain language (CONFIRMED, with the M-P attribution corrected to be sharper)

The structure IS in the real corpus (host PPMI+SVD recovers it, +0.442, all 8 categories). Recovering it from the raw concept×hub counts requires removing the **common mode** — the high-frequency hubs ("said", "day", "big") every concept connects to strongly — which buries the small category-distinguishing residual. Common-mode removal = whitening, and whitening factorizes into two independent operations:

- **The DIAGONAL half is solved, locally, and biologically.** Per-hub mean-centering (subtractive spike-frequency adaptation = `input_mean_adapt`, shipped byte-clean) OR a per-hub divisive gain (`r_h=x_h/(σ+g_h)`, D1) recovers **+0.31 (numpy, the axis-0 ideal) / +0.155 (bridge, E/I-projection-limited) / +0.216 (the D1 divisive-gain form on real)** — a real, biology-canonical, on-substrate escape, but it **plateaus at ~+0.22–0.31, i.e. ~49–70% of host**. This is not a failure of effort: it is the analytic ceiling of *any* diagonal operation on this corpus. The project verified the axis (per-feature/axis-0 is the right one; per-concept/axis-1 caps lower) and the spiking input-mean (Poisson-spiking EMA recovers 96% of the clean mean) — the diagonal is genuinely closed.
- **The OFF-DIAGONAL half is the wall.** The CYCLE-73 probe (`_phaseB_offdiagonal_derisk.py`, 3 seeds, real) is decisive: centering-only (diagonal) **+0.307**; full ZCA whitening (diagonal + off-diagonal) **COLLAPSES to −0.012** (over-whitening — see §3); **low-rank ZCA rank-8 (denoise + decorrelate the top components) = +0.437 ≈ host**; rank-16 +0.382. So the +0.31→+0.44 residual **is real, IS the off-diagonal cross-component decorrelation, and IS closeable — but only by a LOW-RANK (regularized) decorrelation**, not full whitening. The two point-neuron mechanisms tried for it both fall short on real: the point-neuron SM anti-Hebbian lateral (`graded_lateral`) plateaus at **~+0.35** with eff-rank collapsing to 3–4 (over-compression), and the dendritic per-hub divisive gain reaches only **+0.216** (≈ the diagonal, because it *is* the diagonal — §2).

### Does the Mikulasch-Priesemann limit forbid the OFF-DIAGONAL specifically? — YES, and this is the sharpened, corrected attribution.

I verified this against the M-P primary source (PMC8685685), because the project has at times invoked "the M-P limit" for the diagonal too, which is imprecise. The precise reading:

- **M-P is about the OFF-DIAGONAL (cross-neuron) decorrelation, not the diagonal.** The mechanism is that *recurrent connections* `W_jki` (neuron k → compartment i of neuron j) "learn to locally balance specific feedforward inputs on the dendrites" so the population doesn't redundantly re-encode what other neurons already encode. The limitation it proves for point neurons is specifically the *input-specific* inhibition: "learning feedforward synapses Fji correctly is not feasible locally for point neurons, since they lack knowledge of the population code for single inputs xi." That is a statement about removing **between-neuron** redundancy (off-diagonal), not about per-feature scaling.
- **Corollary the project should adopt:** the *diagonal* half (per-feature mean-subtraction) is NOT forbidden by M-P — it is a separable scalar per-neuron operation (point-neuron predictive coding; Lee/Pennartz 2024, PMC11045951), which is exactly why the project could ship `input_mean_adapt` and clear ~+0.31. The M-P wall is **the off-diagonal cross-neuron piece, and only that.** (The CYCLE-71/73 phaseB docs already reached this conclusion independently — "the residual +0.13-to-host is the off-diagonal half"; the M-P primary source confirms it.)
- **The crux for the D2 verdict (§2):** in M-P, the dendritic compartments **carry recurrent inhibition FROM OTHER NEURONS** (the `jki` index is lateral). The compartment is what makes that lateral signal *learnable by a local rule* (it solves the credit-assignment problem of which input to balance). **The compartment does NOT replace the lateral — it localizes it.** A dendritic mechanism that has *no cross-neuron term at all* is therefore not the M-P mechanism and does not do the off-diagonal.

## 2. DOES D2-AS-DESIGNED ADDRESS THE OFF-DIAGONAL? — **NO. The Phase-2 per-compartment divisive gain is provably DIAGONAL-ONLY. D2 as-specified caps at ~49% of host on the real corpus, independent of fixing the Phase-2 NEGATIVE.** (decision-critical)

This is the single most important finding for the owner's build decision, and it is not a judgment call — it is readable directly from the D2 build plan + the D1 code.

### 2.1 What the D2 Phase-2 mechanism actually computes (read from the source)

The D2 build plan (`docs/plans/2026-06-14-D2-dendritic-cortex-build-plan.md`, Phase 2 "Implementation reframe") specifies the cortex code as the **gain-normalized pool activity** — D1's per-hub gain realized on the bridge forward pass:

> "with `enable_dendritic_divisive_gain=True` the common hubs are suppressed at the synaptic input → the pool code reflects the rare informative hubs … the gain is the divisive normalization on the forward read."

The D1 mechanism it instantiates (`dendritic_d1_learn_graded_structure_derisk.py`, the validated runner) is, exactly:

```
learn_perhub_gains:  g_h ← g_h + eta * (x_h − g_h)        # g_h depends ONLY on hub h's own drive
perhub_residual:     r_h = x_h / (sigma + g_h)            # each hub divided by ITS OWN gain
```

Every term is **per-hub, using only that hub's own marginal statistic.** There is **no cross-hub term, no cross-concept term, no lateral, no covariance, anywhere in the mechanism.** In matrix form the operation is `r = D·x` with `D = diag(1/(σ+g_h))` — a **diagonal** transform by construction. A diagonal transform leaves the *correlation structure* (the off-diagonal of the covariance) untouched; it only rescales each axis. **It is mathematically incapable of removing cross-feature correlations.** That is the definition of "diagonal-only."

### 2.2 The empirical confirmation already in hand (the project's own CYCLE-85 probe)

This is not a theoretical worry — the project already measured it. `_phaseB_dendritic_real_derisk.py` (CYCLE 85, the D1 per-hub gain on `build_real_corpus`, 3 seeds):

| arm (real corpus) | Pearson | reading |
|---|---|---|
| host PPMI+SVD | **+0.442** | ceiling |
| DENDRITIC per-hub gain (batch) | **+0.216** | **49% of host — the diagonal ceiling** |
| point-global control | +0.197 | the per-hub gain barely beats it (+0.019) |
| point-neuron SM lateral (ref) | ~+0.35 | the dendritic gain is *below even this* |

The dendritic per-hub gain (+0.216) ≈ centering-only (+0.307 numpy; the difference is divisive-vs-subtractive form) and sits *at the diagonal plateau*, ~0.22 below host. On the *synthetic* corpus the same mechanism reaches +0.845 — but only because the synthetic structure is **diagonal-separable** (common hubs vs category hubs distinguished purely by frequency), so the diagonal alone suffices there. The real corpus is not diagonal-separable: its category structure lives partly in the off-diagonal cross-component correlations, which the divisive gain cannot touch.

### 2.3 The two-compartment-nonlinearity counter-question — could D2's dendrite do the off-diagonal *if it used branch products instead of a divisive gain*? (honest, important nuance)

The prompt rightly asks whether a **two-compartment dendritic nonlinearity (NMDA plateau / branch products)** — as opposed to a divisive gain — could give off-diagonal terms. The literature answer is a **qualified, mostly-no for THIS problem**:

- A dendritic NMDA-plateau nonlinearity DOES compute **products of inputs** (supralinear summation of *spatiotemporally clustered* input on the same branch). In principle a product `x_i·x_j` is a cross-feature (off-diagonal) term. So a branch-product dendrite is *not* diagonal-only in the abstract.
- **BUT** (the load-bearing caveat, from Eyal/Poirazi/Ujfalussy-Lengyel and the 2024 eLife feature-binding work): the dendritic product is computed **only between inputs whose synapses are CO-LOCATED on the same branch**, and the biology *predicts a tight relationship between presynaptic correlation structure and the morphological clustering of synapses* — i.e. you must *already know which features are correlated* and wire those synapses onto a shared branch (or learn that clustering). The dendrite computes products of a **pre-selected, locally-clustered subset**, not the full all-pairs covariance `Σ` that whitening needs. It is a sparse, structured, learned nonlinearity, not a general off-diagonal decorrelator.
- **AND the D2 build does not specify a branch-product nonlinearity anyway** — Phase 2 is explicitly the *divisive gain* (`x/(σ+g_h)`, §2.1), which is the Carandini-Heeger normalization form, not the NMDA-plateau product form. So even granting the abstract possibility, **D2-as-written does not use it.** Converting D2 to a branch-product off-diagonal decorrelator would be a *different, harder, and still-not-the-full-off-diagonal* mechanism — a redesign, not a Phase-2 fix.

### 2.4 The verdict (decision-critical)

**D2-as-designed does NOT address the off-diagonal.** Its Phase-2 mechanism is a per-compartment *divisive gain* = a diagonal transform = the same operation `input_mean_adapt` already ships, just in divisive rather than subtractive form. On the real corpus it is **measured at +0.216 = 49% of host**, the diagonal plateau — and this is a *mathematical* ceiling, so **fixing the current Phase-2 forward-pass NEGATIVE (dendritic +0.188 vs point-neuron +0.287 on synthetic) would at best lift D2 to the diagonal plateau, NOT to host.** The Phase-2 NEGATIVE is a *second, separate* problem (the on-bridge realization is currently below even its own diagonal ceiling); fixing it does not change the architectural cap. **The months-scale D2 build, as specified, cannot reach host on the real corpus** — because it is building the diagonal half, and the real gap is the off-diagonal half. This is the honest, decision-relevant assessment the prompt asked for.

> One-line for the owner: **D2 builds the half that's already solved.** The per-compartment divisive gain is the diagonal; the wall is the off-diagonal cross-neuron decorrelation; the divisive gain cannot do it; ⇒ D2-as-designed caps at ~49% of host on real, NEGATIVE-fix or not.

## 3. RANKED biologically-grounded OPTIONS for the OFF-DIAGONAL

The literature is strikingly convergent: **every** biologically-plausible off-diagonal decorrelator is a **recurrent cross-neuron interaction** (direct anti-Hebbian lateral, or — Dale's-law-cleanly — a separate inhibitory interneuron population, or that lateral delivered locally to dendritic compartments). **There is no purely-local (per-feature, no cross-neuron term) mechanism for the off-diagonal — that is a mathematical fact (a diagonal `D` cannot rotate off-diagonal correlations away), corroborated by every source below.** The options therefore differ not in "local vs non-local" (all are cross-neuron) but in *how* the cross-neuron signal is delivered and regularized, and crucially in whether they **regularize against over-whitening** (the project's full-ZCA-collapses result).

### Option O1 (RECOMMENDED) — a Dale's-law inhibitory-interneuron decorrelating population (King-Zylberberg-DeWeese), LOW-RANK / regularized. Cross-neuron, but POINT neurons.
- **Mechanism.** A separate population of inhibitory interneurons reciprocally connected to the cortex (excitatory) units; the E→I→E loop learns (anti-Hebbian) to cancel the *between-unit* correlations → decorrelated excitatory code. Dale's-law-compliant (no signed direct E-E weights, unlike the project's `graded_lateral` which uses a signed M). To avoid over-whitening, keep it **low-rank**: a *small* interneuron pool (≈ the 8 components the low-rank ZCA keeps) → it whitens the top semantic subspace and leaves the noise subspace alone (this is the King-Zylberberg "add inhibitory cells improves sparse code" result, *bounded*).
- **Biology / citation.** King, Zylberberg, DeWeese, *Inhibitory interneurons decorrelate excitatory cells to drive sparse code formation in a spiking model of V1*, **J. Neurosci.** 2013 (PMC6705060) — the canonical Dale's-law spiking realization of cross-neuron decorrelation; Pehlevan-Chklovskii 2015 (the anti-Hebbian-lateral theory it implements); Lipshutz-Simoncelli, *Shaping the distribution of neural responses with interneurons in a recurrent circuit model*, **NeurIPS** 2024 (arXiv 2405.17745) — a *controllable* target-distribution version (lets you target low-rank/partial whitening rather than full).
- **Local or recurrent lateral?** Recurrent lateral (E↔I), but **point neurons** — no dendritic compartments needed. This is the key advantage over D2.
- **Expected ceiling.** Toward host IF the joint E+I spiking dynamics converge to the low-rank fixed point — but the project's own warning applies: the *existing* `graded_lateral` (the signed-M form of this same rule) **does NOT converge on the bridge** (CYCLE 74–78: eff-rank stuck ~3, never beats the +0.155 random projection; one-step lateral, only one region flagged, joint dynamics don't settle in the streaming window). So O1's risk is identical to the project's already-characterized open piece — it is the *Dale's-law, low-rank, full-recurrent-settle* version of a mechanism that has already plateaued once. **Realistic expectation: numpy can likely hit ~host (it's just low-rank ZCA with an interneuron parameterization); the BRIDGE realization is the unproven, possibly-plateauing piece** (the whole spiking-cortex arc's convergent open problem).

### Option O2 — the Mikulasch-Priesemann dendritic-balance unit (recurrent inhibition delivered to per-input dendritic compartments). Cross-neuron + dendritic.
- **Mechanism.** Each neuron has per-input dendritic compartments; recurrent inhibition `W_jki` (neuron k → compartment i of neuron j) locally cancels the predictable common mode in each compartment via a *local* balance rule; the soma fires from the residual. This is the *faithful* off-diagonal decorrelator — and it is genuinely both cross-neuron (the inhibition is lateral) AND dendritic (the compartment makes the lateral locally learnable).
- **Biology / citation.** Mikulasch, Rudelt, Priesemann, **PNAS** 2021 (PMC8685685); Mikulasch et al., *Dendritic predictive coding*, *Trends in Neurosciences* 2022. The project's catalog G.02 "active dendrites" (MISSING, "one of the largest abstractions in the simulator, ~10× compute/neuron").
- **Local or recurrent lateral?** BOTH — recurrent lateral inhibition, delivered to dendritic compartments. **NOT** "local instead of lateral." This is the precise correction to any reading of D2 as "the dendrite replaces the lateral."
- **Expected ceiling.** Highest fidelity, host-capable in principle (this is *the* theory of how cortex does it). **Cost: the highest** — it needs BOTH a multi-compartment neuron AND a learned recurrent inhibitory matrix (it is O1's interneuron loop PLUS the dendritic localization). It is strictly *more* than the current D2 design, not the same; the current D2 (divisive gain, no lateral) is **not** a step toward O2 — it is missing the entire lateral half. Months-scale, and heavier than the D2 already scoped.

### Option O3 — gain-modulating-interneuron adaptive whitening (Duong-Lipshutz-Chklovskii-Simoncelli), as an analog pre-spike population. Cross-neuron, fixed-weight + plastic gains.
- **Mechanism.** Adaptive whitening via a recurrent circuit of **fixed** synaptic weights + **gain-modulating interneurons** (the marginal-variance route): interneurons adapt their gains (fast) while synapses adapt slowly. A different biological substrate (gain modulation, not plastic lateral weights) for the same cross-neuron decorrelation.
- **Biology / citation.** Duong et al., *Adaptive whitening in neural populations with gain-modulating interneurons*, **ICML** 2023 (arXiv 2301.11955); *…with fast gain modulation and slow synaptic plasticity*, NeurIPS 2023.
- **Local or recurrent lateral?** Recurrent (interneuron) circuit. **No demonstrated SPIKING realization** (rate/graded only — confirmed by this search and the prior dendritic deep-research) → portable as a *rule*, not a turnkey spiking module.
- **Expected ceiling.** Host-capable as a rule, but inherits the over-whitening pathology unless explicitly shaped (the Lipshutz 2024 optimal-transport version is the controllable fix), AND has no spiking realization → lower-readiness than O1. Useful mainly as the *parameterization* that makes the low-rank target controllable.

### The over-whitening caveat that ALL options must respect (the project already discovered it; the literature confirms it is fundamental)
The Pehlevan-Chklovskii theory paper (arXiv 1511.09468) states plainly that pure whitening **"decorrelates the outputs and equalizes their variances"** and **"amplifies noise in low-variance directions."** This is *exactly* the project's full-ZCA-collapse (−0.012) and the SM-lateral over-compression (eff-rank 3–4, plateau +0.35). **⇒ The off-diagonal mechanism must be LOW-RANK / regularized / shaped (keep the top ~8 components, do NOT equalize the noise subspace) — full whitening is the wrong target on this moderate-SNR corpus.** Any option above, run as *full* whitening, will collapse; run as *low-rank* (rank-8 ZCA = +0.437), reaches host. This is the single most actionable design constraint, and it reframes the goal from "whiten" to "low-rank decorrelate the top semantic subspace."

| | Substrate | Cross-neuron? | Spiking realization exists? | Over-whitening control | Cost | Realistic ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **O1 (recommend)** | inhibitory interneuron pool, POINT neurons | yes (E↔I) | yes (King-Z-DeW 2013) | low-rank (small pool) / Lipshutz-shaped | weeks (bridge build; numpy de-risk first) | numpy ~host; **bridge unproven** (= the project's open `graded_lateral` plateau) |
| **O2 (faithful, heaviest)** | dendritic compartments + recurrent inhib | yes (lateral, localized to compartments) | yes (M-P 2021, spiking) | inherent (predictive/balance) | months+ (more than current D2) | host-capable, highest fidelity, highest risk |
| **O3 (rule only)** | gain-modulating interneurons | yes (interneuron circuit) | **no spiking impl** | shaped (Lipshutz 2024 OT) | rate-only port | host as a rule; not spiking-ready |

## 5. THE CHEAP-FIRST numpy DE-RISK (afternoon-scale, NO sim/ edits) — falsify/confirm the top option on the REAL corpus

**The single open question O1 answers:** can a **low-rank, Dale's-law-compatible, full-settle** anti-Hebbian lateral (the interneuron decorrelator) reach the host ceiling on the REAL corpus — beating the diagonal-only +0.22–0.31 AND the existing point-neuron SM lateral +0.35 — *in numpy*, BEFORE any bridge build? (If numpy can't, the bridge certainly can't, and the off-diagonal is confirmed unreachable by this route → ship the flat cortex. If numpy CAN, the remaining risk is purely the bridge spiking realization — the project's already-characterized `graded_lateral`-convergence problem — which is then the sharp, bounded target.)

### 5.1 The toy (smallest thing that falsifies the top option)
- **Input:** `build_real_corpus(seed, n_hub=500)` → the real concept×hub counts `C`, `labels`, a-priori `S_true`. Encode with the diagonal front-end already validated: PPMI (or log + axis-0 centering) → `Xc` (this is the +0.307 diagonal starting point).
- **The off-diagonal learner under test (3 arms, same `Xc`):**
  1. **LOW-RANK ZCA (the target ceiling, host op):** `Xc` whitened keeping rank-8 (the `zca(Xc, rank=8)` already in `_phaseB_offdiagonal_derisk.py`) → confirm +0.437. This is the "what an ideal low-rank off-diagonal gives" reference.
  2. **DALE'S-LAW INTERNEURON LATERAL (the O1 mechanism, numpy):** a *separate* non-negative interneuron pool `I` (size ≈ 8–16), `E→I` and `I→E` weights ≥ 0, the cortex code = `(I + W_EI·W_IE)⁻¹·Xc`-style **full recurrent settle** (the fixed point, not one step — this is what `graded_lateral` is missing), with the interneuron weights learned anti-Hebbian + **bounded/low-rank** (small pool = automatic low rank, avoids over-whitening). The bar: **beat +0.35 (the SM lateral) toward +0.437 (host)**, eff-rank recovers toward ~8 (NOT collapse to 3–4).
  3. **POINT-NEURON DIAGONAL control (must fall short):** the D1 per-hub divisive gain (+0.216) and centering-only (+0.307) — must stay at the diagonal plateau, confirming the lift is the off-diagonal.
- **Generalization:** held-out nearest-category (`heldout_generalization`) above chance for arm 2, at the diagonal-plateau level for arm 3.

### 5.2 The exact anti-cheat controls (mandatory; mirror the deep-research standard)
- **point-neuron-must-fail / diagonal-must-fall-short:** the D1 divisive gain + centering-only stay ≤ +0.31 (the diagonal plateau) WHILE the interneuron lateral exceeds +0.35 toward host. *The contrast IS the result.*
- **host-carries:** PPMI+SVD on the same `C` = +0.44 (else corpus issue). Low-rank-ZCA rank-8 = +0.437 confirms the off-diagonal target is real.
- **permuted-similarity-collapses:** shuffle which concepts are same-category → arm-2 Pearson AND generalization → ~0.
- **lesion:** zero the interneuron pool (or freeze `M` to identity) → the off-diagonal lift collapses back to the diagonal plateau → proves the lift rides the lateral, not a code property.
- **over-whitening guard (the project-specific one):** report eff-rank — full-rank whitening must show the collapse (−0.012, eff-rank→noise), low-rank must hold ~8. The lateral must NOT equalize the noise subspace (the M-ratio/bounded-M guard from `_A_whitening_learn_lateral_derisk.py`: `‖M_learned‖/‖M_analytic‖` near 1, not 9000×).
- **S_true a-priori:** assert `s_true_independent` (constructed taxonomy, never corpus-derived).
