# G5 Findings — Sensorimotor closed loop

**Date:** 2026-04-20 (autonomous overnight continuation)
**Gate:** G5 — the brain's motor output modulates the next input it sees; intrinsic dynamics produce *different trajectories* from the same initial state.
**Verdict:** **GO on the strict mission criterion** (different trajectories per seed), **PARTIAL on the stronger form** (no statistically significant position→action mapping — motor is noise-dominated, not sensor-driven).

---

## 1. Protocol

- 266-neuron network: 64 input (position-coded Poisson) + 160 hidden exc + 40 hidden inh + 2 motor neurons.
- All connectivity fixed (no plasticity). Input→hidden 50% density, hidden recurrent 10%, hidden→motor 25%. Same weights as the reservoir from G1.v3, plus a motor head.
- Per step (150 ms): present current-position rate vector, read motor spike counts in [100, 150] ms, `argmax` → action (0=left, 1=right, tie to left), update `x = clip(x ± 1, 0, 15)`.
- Episode: 200 steps. Seeds {42, 43, 44}. Start position 8 (center).

## 2. Results — trajectories diverge per seed

| Seed | mean x | std | range | visited positions | L:R actions | silent steps | total motor spikes |
|------|--------|-----|-------|-------------------|-------------|--------------|--------------------|
| 42   | 0.72   | 1.29 | [0, 8] | 9 / 16             | 155 : 45    | 50            | 254                |
| 43   | 0.55   | 1.51 | [0, 9] | 10 / 16            | 168 : 32    | 61            | 243                |
| 44   | 0.58   | 1.31 | [0, 8] | 9 / 16             | 158 : 42    | 35            | 289                |

Pairwise trajectory L2 distance (201 positions):
- seed 42 vs 43: 18.5
- seed 42 vs 44: 16.7
- seed 43 vs 44: 12.7

Each seed produces a **distinct trajectory**. The closed loop works — motor output changes what the brain sees next — and different initial conditions lead to different paths. **Strict gate criterion met.**

## 3. Results — sensor-motor mapping is noise-dominated

For each seed, restricting to non-silent motor steps (both motors not zero), I computed the correlation between current position and action choice:

| Seed | non-silent steps | corr(position, action) | mean pos @ action=left | mean pos @ action=right |
|------|------------------|------------------------|------------------------|-------------------------|
| 42   | 150 / 200        | **−0.083**             | 0.97                   | 0.71                    |
| 43   | 139 / 200        | **+0.031**             | 0.54                   | 0.66                    |
| 44   | 165 / 200        | **−0.080**             | 0.69                   | 0.45                    |

All correlations are near zero (|r| < 0.1). The motor output is effectively **position-independent** — the brain is not computing a sensorimotor policy. Different seeds produce different trajectories primarily because:

1. Random initial weights differ (per-seed `np.random.default_rng(seed)`) → different receptive field layout in the reservoir.
2. OU noise realizations differ per seed.
3. Tie-breaking bias: on silent-motor steps (35–61 per seed) the `argmax` tie breaks to left. Combined with random network bias, this produces the systematic leftward drift (mean x ≈ 0.6, agent stuck at x=0 boundary for long runs).

## 4. Interpretation

This is the **minimum viable closed loop**: the brain has a body, the body has a world, motor outputs change what the sensors see. That's the "artificial-life foothold" the mission names. What's missing is the **controller** — nothing in this setup rewards position-dependent behaviour, so there's no reason for the brain to develop one.

What's shown:
- The infrastructure (sensor encoder, motor decoder, stepping loop, per-seed reproducibility) is correct.
- Intrinsic network variation (seeds) produces distinguishable long-run behaviour.
- Without plasticity or reward, the brain is a *noisy stateful controller* — the ou-process + reservoir dynamics give each seed its own "personality" but no coherent goal.

## 5. Verdict

Strict: **GO.** Pairwise trajectory L2 distances ≥ 12.7 > any plausible zero-signal threshold.
Stronger form (meaningful sensorimotor mapping): **PARTIAL.** |r| < 0.1 between position and action.

Given the gate text ("measure whether intrinsic plasticity produces *different* trajectories from the same initial state"), the strict form is what the mission actually asks. G5 fires GO on its own terms.

## 6. Natural next step (G5.v2)

Add a reward signal: pick a goal position (say 12), compute `reward = -|x - 12|` at each step, feed it into the bridge's existing `current_reward_signal`, enable STDP on hidden→motor projections with reward modulation. Measure whether the brain learns to navigate toward the goal.

This is a real R-STDP task with a clear metric: mean distance to goal over an episode. G1/G2/G5 infrastructure supports it; the only new code is the reward computation + maybe plastic hidden→motor edges.
