## Per-benchmark final verdicts

### sequential_expansion ✓ (3/3 PASS)

Train each word once, expand vocab, test all. Mean 0.95 ± 0.06.
Range 0.88-1.00.

This is the same problem as Phase 1.4 BRANCH A's no-catastrophic-forgetting
benchmark, just at suite scale. Reproduces the Phase 1.4 5/6 result.
**Vocab GROWTH works at scaled arch.**

### retention_over_time ✓ (3/3 PASS)

Train, freeze plasticity for 5K silent steps, retest. Mean 0.94 ± 0.07.
Range 0.87-1.00.

This is the same mechanism as Phase 1.3 hippocampus consolidation
3/3 strict anti-cheat result, minus the hippocampus replay step.
**Cortex-internal recurrence retains binding without external
maintenance.**

### interference ✗ (0/3 PASS, ceiling characterized)

Train all 8 synonym words INTERLEAVED, test all 8. Mean 0.39 ± 0.05.
Per-word bimodal pattern persists across all hypothesis tests:

```
                v200/n=1000   v400/n=1000   v400/n=2000

  north         0.52          0.60          0.60
  east          0.56          0.44          0.68
  west          0.32          0.40          0.52
```

Same words consistently win (north/east/west) AND lose (right/down/up/left).
Suggests per-word DRIVE PATTERN COLLISION under sparse `vocab_to_drive_pattern`
encoding — words with overlapping bit-patterns compete for the same
motor weights during interleaved training and weak-pattern words lose.

## Why demote not invest more

Three reasons:

1. **Both tunable levers exhausted at single-seed.** Three specific
   testable hypotheses across two benchmarks, all REFUTED with
   small-but-sub-threshold lifts. Going to multi-seed at any of these
   variants would just confirm the partial lift; it wouldn't produce
   pass-grade scores.

2. **The bimodal per-word pattern is consistent.** Same words win/lose
   across both benchmarks (interference and long_tail). Drive-pattern
   collision is the most parsimonious explanation. Fixing it requires
   architectural change (orthogonal drive patterns, decoupled
   per-word eligibility, sequential-not-interleaved curriculum) not
   a tuning sweep.

3. **The biology-grounded interleaved + few-shot regimes are
   genuinely hard problems.** Real biology has the same limits — 50
   events is ~13s of speech; critical periods need months. The
   architecture isn't broken; it's hitting a real boundary.

## Strategic implication for master plan

Phase 1.5 was scoped as "biology-grounded continual learning validated
at multi-seed across 4 stress benchmarks". That's now characterized
empirically:

- 2 of 4 benchmarks (sequential + retention) PASS at multi-seed.
  These are the SEQUENTIAL CONTINUAL LEARNING REGIME. Validated.
- 2 of 4 benchmarks (interference + long_tail) FAIL with
  architectural ceilings. These are INTERLEAVED + FEW-SHOT REGIMES.
  Not validated; defined as boundary.

The master plan thesis ("biology-grounded continual learning works
at scaled arch") **is supported for the sequential regime AND
specifically NOT supported for the interleaved/few-shot regimes**.
Honest documentation of the empirical capability boundary.

**Pivot: Phase 2.2b 10M-param overnight on path-f-hybrid branch.**
Phase 2 tests an orthogonal mechanism (gradient-based pretraining
for character-level language modeling). It doesn't depend on
Phase 1.5 outcomes — they probe different things.

## Open research clusters (parked, not blocked)

The Phase 1.5 ceiling defines two future research clusters:

### Cluster: interleaved binding fix
- Orthogonal vs random drive patterns (eliminate collision)
- Decoupled per-word eligibility traces (separate learning trajectories)
- Curriculum: 4-word base then add 4 synonyms incrementally (sequential vs interleaved)
- Different plasticity rule (gated by drive-pattern overlap)

### Cluster: few-shot binding fix
- Synaptic tag-and-capture (eligibility persistence beyond eligibility window)
- Replay during rare-word training (consolidation-style for rare events)
- Dedicated few-shot pool (architectural separation from common-word pool)
- Schedule: spaced repetition rather than dense ratio of rare-vs-common

Both are weeks of research. Neither blocks Phase 2.
