# 2026-05-09 — Phase 1.5 n_motor=2000 interference: capacity hypothesis REFUTED

**Status:** Single-seed test of `phase_1_5_interference_only_n_motor_2000`
(seed 42). Both dose AND capacity hypotheses for interference are now
REFUTED. The interference failure is robust across both tunable levers.

## Verdict

**Both Phase 1.5 hypotheses for interference REFUTED:**

1. **2026-05-09 morning hypothesis (under-training):** v200 → v400
   produced +0.005 score change. Doubling event count did not lift
   interleaved binding.

2. **2026-05-09 evening hypothesis (capacity):** v400 n_motor=1000 →
   v400 n_motor=2000 produced +0.045 score change. Doubling motor
   pool size did not lift interleaved binding above the 0.50 threshold.

The interference failure is **architectural** AND **not capacity-bound
at the same scale-up that fixed 12-word vocab**. The same n_motor
scale-up that produced 12-word vocab default 71% → scaled 100% (the
2026-05-09 capacity-hypothesis CONFIRMED multi-seed) does NOT
translate to 8-word interleaved interference at this magnitude.

## What this DOES tell us

The **bimodal per-word pattern persists across all 3 runs**:

- **Strong words** (north 0.60, east 0.44-0.68, west 0.40-0.52) bind
  cleanly. They lift slightly with bigger architecture.
- **Weak words** (right 0.08-0.20, down 0.16-0.24, up 0.20-0.32, left
  0.24-0.36) cluster near or below chance. They do NOT lift much
  with 5x more events OR 2x more neurons.

The pattern suggests:
- Per-word DRIVE PATTERN COLLISION under sparse vocab_to_drive_pattern
  encoding. Words that get assigned overlapping or competing language_input
  neurons fight for the same motor-pool weights during interleaved
  training; the network settles on the strongest-driving words.
- Random-init asymmetry: which words become "strong" varies but `right`
  and `down` consistently struggle, possibly due to specific drive-
  pattern bit overlaps with neighbors.

## What this DOES NOT tell us

- Whether even larger n_motor (4000, 8000) would lift interference.
  Diminishing returns suggest no, but we haven't tested.
- Whether a different vocab_to_drive_pattern allocation (e.g., orthogonal
  patterns rather than random sparse) would change the bimodal pattern.
- Whether sequential training (curriculum: 4-word base then add 4
  synonyms incrementally) would lift interference. Phase 1.4 BRANCH A
  explored this for forgetting; not directly tested for interference.
