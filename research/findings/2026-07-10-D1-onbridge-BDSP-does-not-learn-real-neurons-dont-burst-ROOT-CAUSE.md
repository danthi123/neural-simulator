## The root cause (a0 read of the substrate + the reference)
The committed BDSP credit is `dw ∝ Etilde·(B − Pbar·E) = Etilde·E·(P − Pbar)`, where **B = burst rate = a 2nd spike within `burst_isi_threshold_ms` (6 ms ≈ 166 Hz instantaneous).** On the real `SimulationBridge` the output neurons are **regular-spiking (RS) Izhikevich**: they fire single spikes (events, E>0) but almost never a fast doublet, so **measured B ≈ 0** (0.000 at rest, 0.087 even with a hard apical). With B≈0 the credit collapses to ≈ `−Pbar·E` (the class-independent event-rate baseline) — the apical error the credit is supposed to carry is swamped. No burst signal → no directed learning → cannot even overfit.

**Reconciliation with the 0.964 "on spikes" result** (`2026-07-07-D1-microcircuit-...-on-spikes.md`, `_gnw_d1_spiking_bdsp_derisk.py`): the `MicrocircuitBDSPNet` that reaches 0.964 computes E and P as **analytic sigmoids** (`_sig(acts @ W)`), so its burst quantity `B = P·E` is a **smooth analytic product that ALWAYS carries the apical error** — there is no "B≈0" failure in that abstraction. The doc is explicit that the REAL-`SimulationBridge` check (Stage A) validated only the **burst DETECTOR + apical→P read** ("driving the apical raises the measured burst rate B while E is ~invariant"), NOT end-to-end learning-to-accuracy. ⇒ **the deep-credit rule learns in the spike-COUNT abstraction; the end-to-end learning has never been demonstrated on the real spiking substrate**, and this runner is the first to try it to accuracy — and it exposes the real gap.
