## Architecture bug

Phase 1.4 v1 used Tier 2.1 v4 SCALE-UP arch (4096/1000/120) but
Tier 1 BREAKTHROUGH validated baseline was at STANDARD arch
(2048/500/60). For 4-word vocab at scale-up, the architecture was
over-provisioned -- too many synapses to differentiate per word at
200 events.

**Validated Tier 1 v2 at standard arch (2026-05-06):**

| Seed | W->A | A->W |
|---|---|---|
| 42 | 33% | 46% |
| 43 | 45% | 44% |
| 44 | 36% | 35% |
| 100 | 34% | 44% |
| 101 | 40% | 54% |
| 102 | 38% | 47% |
| Mean | 38% | 45% |

**Phase 1.4 v1 smoke at scale-up arch (this run):**

| Phase | W->A |
|---|---|
| A (4 words, 200 events each) | 14% |
| B (4 synonyms only, 200 events each) | 18% (primary retention) |

The 14% Phase A is 24pp below the validated 38% baseline at standard
arch. Cause: scale-up arch was developed for 8-word synonym training
where two synonyms per action provide co-firing diversity that
stabilizes binding. With only 4 words, motor pools at scale-up
arch don't get enough discriminative training.

---

## What the v1 result still tells us

**Despite the architecture bug, v1 has positive signal:**

1. **No catastrophic forgetting**: primary retention went from 14%
   (post-A) to 18% (post-B) -- a 4pp INCREASE, not decrease.
   Synonym training did not erase primary bindings.

2. **Vocabulary diversity helps**: training synonyms (which share
   motor pool targets with primaries) reinforced the motor pool
   representations. This is biologically expected: motor_N getting
   more events (from "north" + "up") strengthens its dynamics.

3. **Architecture matters more than feared**: a too-large architecture
   actually HURTS learning. Less is more for sparse small vocab.

These three findings, while preliminary at single-seed, are
encouraging for Path F's biology premise.

## Lesson

CLAUDE.md flagged the standard-vs-scale-up arch distinction
clearly: "Tier 2.1 v4 scale-up arch (validated 5/6 + 6/6)" was
specifically about SCALE-UP. But the original Phase 1.4
implementation copied scale-up defaults assuming bigger = better.
Lesson: validate baseline accuracy before running multi-phase tests.
A single-seed Phase A eval BEFORE Phase B would have caught this
in 20 minutes instead of 55.

Future continual-learning tests should always include a "Phase A
sanity check" with explicit pass criterion (>= chance accuracy
within X% of historical baseline) before committing to Phase B.
