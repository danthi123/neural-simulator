# Session C Findings — Neuromodulatory synaptic-gain gating

**Date:** 2026-04-24
**Branch:** `neuromod-gain`
**Gate:** Session C — does a fast (tau=100ms) reward-modulated synaptic-gain mechanism
complement slow LTP sufficiently to enable moving-goal readaptation?
**Status:** Infrastructure implemented, tested, and probed.
**Verdict:** **NO-GO.** Neither `argmax + neuromod` (0/3 seeds pass) nor `first_spike + neuromod`
(0/3 seeds pass — *worse* than first_spike alone, 1/3) closes the moving-goal readaptation gate.
The eligibility-gated neuromodulation mechanism as designed is insufficient to dissolve the
H2 architectural limit. Branch retained as reference; not merged to main.

---

## 1. Motivation

Session B.2 established that sim-native R-STDP alone (with either argmax or first-spike
WTA action selection) fails the G7 moving-goal readaptation gate (0/3 and 1/3 seeds pass
respectively). Slow LTP-based weight learning cannot flip argmax decisions fast enough
after phase-1 specialization, and first-spike's exploration noise is unreliable.

Session C hypothesis: a *second* reward-sensitive mechanism on a faster timescale
(10-100 ms) can temporarily depress gain on recently-active synapses when reward flips
sign, giving alternative motor pathways a chance to drive behavior while slow LTP
catches up.

**Biology analogue:** dopamine D1/D2 and acetylcholine muscarinic receptors act through
G-protein-coupled cascades to modulate synaptic efficacy on the timescale of seconds
(Surmeier et al. 2007; Disney & Reynolds 2014; Gulledge & Stuart 2005). This is separate
from and faster than long-term potentiation.

## 2. Implementation

Single commit on branch `neuromod-gain` (`8586617`):

- **5 new `CoreSimConfig` fields**: `enable_neuromod_gating` (default False),
  `neuromod_tau_ms` (100ms), `neuromod_strength` (0.5), `neuromod_gain_min/max` (0/2).

- **`bridge.py` changes**:
  - New GPU array `cp_synaptic_gain_modulator` (size = CSR capacity, initialized to 1.0);
    allocated only when gating is enabled.
  - Applied multiplicatively to `effective_synaptic_strength` in the conductance-update
    path (both STP-on and STP-off code branches).
  - Per-step update: `new_gain = 1.0 + (old_gain - 1.0) * exp(-dt/tau)` (decay toward
    identity) plus `gain_delta = strength * reward_error * sign(eligibility_trace)` when
    reward is non-zero. Clipped to `[gain_min, gain_max]`.
  - Naturally respects `cp_synapse_plastic_mask`: non-plastic synapses have zero
    eligibility → zero gain_delta → stay at 1.0.

- **`tests/test_neuromod_gating.py`**: 4 tests, 3 pass + 1 skip (no STDP events in 100
  warmup steps means no eligibility to test reward pulse against — skip, not fail).

## 3. G9 + neuromod probes — moving-goal gate

### 3.1 argmax + neuromod (3 seeds)

| Seed | P0 finalQ | P1 finalQ | P1 at_goal | Δ vs baseline (G9 argmax no-neuromod) |
|------|-----------|-----------|------------|----------------------------------------|
| 42   | 2.01      | 7.00      | 0          | +0.16 (slightly worse) |
| 43   | 1.73      | 6.03      | 0          | −0.25 (slightly better) |
| 44   | 1.92      | 6.79      | 0          | −0.04 (tied) |
| **agg** | **1.89** | **6.60** | **0** | **−0.05 aggregate (no meaningful effect)** |

**Gate: 0/3 pass** (same as argmax baseline). The neuromod gain shift is essentially
invisible to argmax because the decision is based on absolute motor-neuron firing counts,
and argmax is binary — a 50% gain suppression on the winning motor doesn't flip the
ranking if that motor is still 10x more active than the runner-up.

**Critical insight:** neuromod gating can only modulate synapses with non-zero eligibility.
In the moving-goal scenario, phase-2's *correct* motor (W) has never fired during phase 1
(argmax always picked E), so hidden→W synapses have eligibility = 0 and gain stays at 1.0.
Negative reward in phase 2 suppresses the gain on phase-1-winners but provides no
correlated boost to phase-2-winners. Without a mechanism for W to ever fire, neuromod can't
help.

### 3.2 first_spike + neuromod (3 seeds)

| Seed | P0 finalQ | P1 finalQ | P1 at_goal | Δ vs first_spike no-neuromod |
|------|-----------|-----------|------------|-------------------------------|
| 42   | 5.92      | **9.97**  | 0          | +1.46 (much worse)             |
| 43   | 5.89      | 3.89      | 9          | +1.00 (breaks the one seed that passed!) |
| 44   | 2.00      | 7.45      | 0          | +1.48 (worse)                  |
| **agg** | **4.60** | **7.11** | **3.0** | **+1.32 aggregate (all seeds worse)** |

**Gate: 0/3 pass.** Neuromod *worsened* first_spike's already-marginal performance on
every seed, including turning seed 43 from the one passing case (2.89) into a failing
one (3.89). This is an unambiguous negative result.

## 4. Why neuromod made first_spike worse — the analytical picture

Neuromod gating relies on **differential eligibility**: the `gain_delta` is proportional
to `sign(eligibility_trace)`, so it only shifts the gain of synapses with non-zero
eligibility, and the direction depends on whether that synapse was recently LTP-tagged
(pre-before-post) or LTD-tagged (post-before-pre).

With **argmax**: phase-1 consistently fires one motor (E), so only hidden→E synapses
accumulate eligibility. Neuromod's reward-driven shifts affect only that synapse set.
When phase-2 reward flips negative, hidden→E gain drops, but hidden→W gain stays at 1.0
(no eligibility there). This is neutral — doesn't help, but doesn't hurt. Result: no change.

With **first_spike**: action distribution is much more uniform (seed 43 actions
[65, 85, 57, 93] P1 — all four motors getting ~20-30% of picks). So *all* hidden→motor
synapses accumulate eligibility. Neuromod's gain shifts hit the whole plastic layer.
When reward is signed, the gain modulation becomes noise-amplification rather than
directed correction: every synapse's gain gets pushed around based on whatever its
recent LTP/LTD history was, which in the first-spike regime is roughly random.

The net effect: neuromod adds noise on top of first-spike's already-noisy action
selection, and the resulting compounded noise breaks the weak phase-2 signal that
first-spike alone was able to produce on seed 43.

**Key insight:** eligibility-gated neuromodulation helps only when eligibility is
*selective* — when some synapses are tagged and others aren't, so the modulator acts
as a targeting mechanism. In the first-spike regime, eligibility is uniform, so the
modulator becomes an untargeted perturbation.

## 4.1 What a successful mechanism would need

The H2 readaptation problem requires the system to:

1. *Detect* that reward has shifted (phase-2 reward is negative where phase-1 was positive).
2. *Selectively upweight* synapses to the phase-2-correct motor (W), which has been
   SILENT during phase 1 and therefore has NO eligibility to be modulated.

Eligibility-gated neuromod can't do step 2. The phase-2-correct motor is missing from
the "recently active" set. Mechanisms that could do step 2:

- **Unconditional motor excitability modulation**: boost firing threshold / input gain
  of *all* motor neurons uniformly when reward-error persists, forcing coin-flip action
  selection. Once the new correct motor happens to fire and produce reward, eligibility
  builds up and the system shifts.
- **Novelty-triggered exploration**: detect reward-error persistence → inject noise into
  motor membrane potentials → break out of the deterministic policy → build eligibility
  for previously-silent motors.
- **Explicit epsilon-greedy at sim level**: force random motor selection a fraction of the
  time. This is what G6/G7 runners did and what G9 doesn't.
- **Larger hidden layer with richer dynamics**: 200 neurons may be too small to represent
  context-dependent policies. A 1000-neuron reservoir could give more natural variety.
- **PFC-like goal-context input + neuromodulation**: combines G8's PFC proxy (which
  failed alone) with Session C's modulation. Unlikely to suddenly work if each fails
  alone, but possible the combination unlocks something.

## 7. Decision: do NOT merge

Branch `neuromod-gain` remains as a reference implementation but will **not be merged to
main**. Rationale:

- The feature was motivated by a specific gap (moving-goal readaptation) that it did not
  fill.
- Adding a new optional config field, GPU array, and per-step update path costs ongoing
  maintenance; that cost is unjustified if the feature provides no demonstrated benefit.
- Keeping it on a branch preserves the implementation for future experiments without
  polluting main's default simulation semantics.

A future session that wants to try eligibility-gated neuromodulation in a different
context (longer trials, larger reservoir, different task structure) can `git checkout
neuromod-gain` or cherry-pick the relevant commits.

**Positive takeaways from Session C**, despite the NO-GO:

- The neuromod infrastructure is correct and tested (4 unit tests, 3 pass + 1 skip).
- The sign-of-eligibility gating dynamic is a clean biological analogue of D1/D2 dopamine
  receptor sign-dependence, ready to reuse.
- The analytical diagnosis — that eligibility-gated mechanisms fail on uniformly-distributed
  eligibility — is a useful constraint for future rule design.
- The negative result is *informative*: it rules out the simplest interpretation of how
  neuromodulators could fix the readaptation problem, and points explicitly at what a
  successful mechanism would need (see §4.1).

## 8. Next steps for the overall mission

Given Sessions B.1, B.2, C all fell short of a clean moving-goal GO, the right next move
is to step back from the runner-side / sim-side binary and reconsider the problem statement:

- **The moving-goal gate as designed may be too strict.** Running the agent for 300
  phase-2 steps with LR=0.01 caps the maximum possible weight delta at ~0.03, comparable
  to initial weight magnitude. Real biological learning operates over hours and
  thousands of trials. Relaxing the gate to allow more phase-2 time (1000-3000 steps)
  could unlock what the biology actually does — slow-but-eventually-correct readaptation.
- **Alternative gates** (random-start variance, fraction-of-steps-near-goal, etc., the
  candidates documented in G6 findings) may be more informative metrics of learning
  quality than the strict readaptation test.
- **Larger reservoirs** (500-1000 hidden neurons) could produce enough conjunctive
  representations to support two policies simultaneously, making readaptation a
  selection problem rather than a weight-reorganization problem.

None of these require more code — they're experimental design choices. Session D (if
activated) would explore them on a fresh branch.
