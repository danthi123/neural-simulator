# G7 Findings — Moving-goal sensorimotor readaptation

**Date:** 2026-04-21 (autonomous continuation)
**Gate:** G7 — swap goal mid-episode; does the agent readapt? Tests whether the G5.v3/G6 signed-perceptron rule produces a real position-dependent policy or a specialization to one task instance.
**Verdict:** **NO-GO across three rule variants (B, B+eps-reset, C).** The runner-side signed-perceptron approach, regardless of update-rule details, cannot shed phase-1 weight specialization fast enough to adapt when the goal moves at step 300. This is a **genuine architectural finding**, not a bug. Moving to Alternative I (signed eligibility in the sim) on a branch as the principled next step.

---

## 1. Protocol

- 8×8 grid, start `(1, 1)`, goal 1 `(6, 6)` for steps 0–299, goal 2 `(2, 5)` for steps 300–599. Manhattan distance from (6,6) to (2,5) = 5.
- 266-neuron reservoir (same as G6), signed perceptron on hidden→motor.
- `learning_rate = 0.01`, `lr_schedule = decay_after_goal` (reset on goal change, so phase 2 starts with full LR).
- Epsilon-greedy exploration (G6.v2 default: start=0.3, end=0.05, decay=150/200).
- `strict_step_errors = True`.
- Gate: phase-2 final-quarter mean distance < 3.0 in ≥ 2/3 seeds. Random baseline for Manhattan distance on 8×8 is ~7.

**Rule C actually regressed slightly.** Seed 43's phase-2 finalQ went 3.09 (v1) → 3.28 (v2) → 4.15 (v3). Even with the oracle telling the agent "go W," it still picks W only ~10 times out of 300 for seeds 42 and 44.

## 3. Interpretation

The issue is not the direction of the gradient signal. It's that the gradient magnitude is **too small to move weights past the phase-1-specialized attractor**:

- After 300 steps of phase 1, plastic weights `(hidden_i → N motor)` and `(hidden_i → E motor)` for "starting-region" hidden neurons are saturated near `w_max=3.0`. Weights to W and S are near 0.
- Phase 2 LR is `0.01` (full strength, decay resets on goal change). Max weight change per synapse per step is `0.01 × h_act × 1.0` where `h_act` ∈ [0, 1] normalized. Practically ~0.005 per update per synapse.
- Over 300 phase-2 steps, max cumulative change ≈ 1.5 weight units. Starting weights are 3.0 (wrong motors) and 0.0 (right motors). Getting W above E requires moving both by ~1.5 units each in opposite directions — exactly at the ceiling of what the rule can achieve in 300 steps.
- **argmax is cliff-edged.** Until W weight exceeds E weight for enough hidden inputs, the agent keeps picking E, getting reward=0 or -1, applying tiny corrective deltas, and keeps picking E. Self-reinforcing policy inertia.

Rule C doesn't help because it still operates at the same LR scale with the same argmax decision rule. Even an oracle-correct gradient can't move a weight 3× its per-step step-size.

## 4. Why this is useful

G7 cleanly separates "learning" from "specialization":

- **G5.v3 and G6 v1/v2** demonstrated that the signed-perceptron approach *learns* a single sensorimotor task.
- **G7** demonstrates that what it learns is a *specialization*, not a *controller*. The policy is tied to one goal location; moving the goal breaks it.

This is exactly the behaviour the artificial-life mission should be suspicious of. "The brain learned to navigate" is a stronger claim than "the brain learned to navigate to one specific place." G7's NO-GO is thus a *correct negative result* that identifies what's missing.

## 5. What's needed — Alternative I: signed eligibility in the sim

The sim's current STDP/reward-modulation path uses an **unsigned eligibility trace** (`|Δw|` accumulates in `cp_eligibility_trace`). This is why G5.v2 originally failed (negative reward uniformly depresses → weights collapse) and why my runner-side workaround was needed in the first place.

The principled fix:
1. Change `fused_stdp_weight_update` (or the eligibility-accumulation line in `_run_one_simulation_step`) to store **signed** Δw in the trace: `cp_eligibility_trace[idx] += weight_changes` instead of `+= cp.abs(weight_changes)`.
2. Re-validate every biological benchmark (Pavlovian associative conditioning, R-STDP gridworld, STDP timing curve, gamma oscillations, E/I balance, STP paired-pulse, homeostasis). Any that changes behaviour meaningfully gets a science-judgment call on whether the new behaviour is still "biologically correct."
3. If all benchmarks pass, the sim has native signed R-STDP and the runner-side perceptron hack can be retired. Gates G5.v3 / G6 / G7 can be rerun using the sim's own reward-modulation path. The weight dynamics will naturally decay old associations when the sign of reward flips at goal change, because individual signed eligibility entries can be *cancelled* by opposite-direction updates.

**This work goes on a branch.** Operator's constraint: "don't ruin the biological-science work." I can do the engineering; the science-judgment on the Pavlovian/R-STDP benchmarks is a review the operator needs to make.

## 6. Alternative II: accept the limit, pick a different gate

If signed eligibility is too science-invasive, the other route is to choose gates that the current approach can pass:

- **G7.alt**: single fixed goal, measure generalization across starting positions (option C from yesterday's G7 candidates). Seeds {42, 43, 44} all start from uniform-random cells; averaged over 30 episodes, measure mean distance. If the policy truly encodes position-dependent outputs (not just a "go NE" heuristic), it should handle varied starts.
- **G7.alt2**: fraction-of-steps-near-goal metric (option A from yesterday). Upgrades G6 PARTIAL to likely GO without new runs.

These don't require sim changes, but they also don't address the real limitation exposed by G7: the policy specializes.
