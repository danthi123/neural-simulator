**Costs (both must be named bars in the TEST prereg):**
1. **Self-name overwrite.** On the 18 "What's your name?" probes, K-P answers "My name is Dan" (the user's name) 8/18 times
   vs B 0/18, and names itself Nova 1/18 vs 7/18. It also writes "Your cat is called Dan". Name overwrite is a listed prereg
   class that B already shows in other forms, so the letter passes. The rate is sharply higher.
2. **Stock-refusal collapse nights.** K-P has whole probe nights of "I'm sorry, I don't know what you mean": s321 intact
   n15, s320 hippocampus-off n11-12, s319 hippocampus-off n15. That is 59 of 1158 intact plus hippocampus-off rows, vs 0 for
   B. Net correct answers are still far higher (intact 95/71/69 vs B 50/53/43 of 148). By lens (b) this is an
   abstention rise, so a default flip would need a ruling even after TEST.
   Also seen in the transcripts:
   - more generic assistant prose in the lived arms: numbered tips and "Sure, I'd be happy to help", 8 replies;
   - follow-up questions;
   - "I've seen it" experience phrasings, 30 per arm vs 0;
   - in both-off, fluent confabulation where B refused (invented 55 vs 22-27).

**Confounds and caveats.**
- **Device.** K-P ran CUDA fp16 and B ran CPU fp32. The precision refuter finds that fp16 does not explain the effect:
  - frozen NLL differs by 1.4e-4 between devices;
  - the PR-x fp16 lives track CPU B at 0.54-1.09x;
  - the effect is a growth-rate divergence, not an offset;
  - the CPU-only one-night gate repeats it on 3 seeds (0.27-0.31x).
  It is not isolated on a K-P life, though. Three B lives on AWS fp16 (`_scratch/stage2/launch_bg.sh`, `lifeBg_s<S>`) were
  launched to close it.
- **Host records.** `tools/where_ran.py` reports host unknown. The JSON `host` and `device` fields carry it.
- **Sample.** One life per arm per seed, DEV only.
- **Canaries.** Only 4 of the 6 could be planted for K-P (no wrong-episode or perspective site). Recall was 4/4 for both readers.
