**Reading (prereg, GPU vs GPU).** 2.9B >= 15/72 on the registered rule, and worse than 1.5B on the open-turn count too
(16 vs 6): scale is not the lever in this range. The 2.9B loops take the form of a non-answer template carried into
open turns: "What do you mean?" x15 (script 1115), "I'm not sure. Can you tell..." x11-12 on every sample of script
1113, "I don't know." x15. It also asks the user's questions back ("What's the name of your hedgehog?" x15). The
0.4B -> 1.5B halving does not continue. Two scales of one model family, one checkpoint each: a verdict on "a bigger
cortex of this series" as the method, not on scale in general.

**Device.** 1.5B loops less on the AWS GPU (fp16) than on the pool CPU (fp32): 10 vs 18 on the registered rule, same
scripts and sampling seeds. The sampled streams differ by device, so part of this is sampling noise (72 conversations
each). But it is large enough that pool (CPU) and AWS (GPU) collapse rates must not be pooled. This may explain why
pool and AWS disagreed earlier this arc (e.g. reply-only rehearsal recall).

**Next.** Per the prereg: the collapse belongs to the format or the sampling, and the next method targets that. The
brain keeps the 1.5B cortex. Open: a 1.5B day-2 conversation on GPU loops in 6/72 conversations on open turns, so the
residual defect at the decision host is smaller than the pool runs suggested.
